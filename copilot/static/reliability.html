GATE 2

Is the instrument telling the truth?

A drifting sensor and a genuinely changing process produce the same symptom — the alert count moves. They demand opposite responses. The discriminator is physics that must hold at any operating point: the process does not control ambient temperature, so a thermal shift with steady mechanics can only be the instrument.

GATE 3

Is the rule still valid?

The dangerous drift is not the sensor — it is the rule silently becoming wrong. A tool supplier changes and the real overstrain limit moves; every margin is then verifiably computed, and wrong. Two counters catch it with no model and no retraining: a failure at positive margin means the threshold is too loose, an alert with no failure means too tight. DISCOVERY

The rules are recoverable from data alone

The fair criticism of a physics-first system is that the dataset handed us its thresholds. It did not have to. Bracketing between the largest non-failing value and the smallest failing one estimates a deterministic boundary from outcomes — and the width of that bracket is the honest uncertainty. L lands within 0.01% on 87 supporting failures; H within 3.4% on two. That is precisely why a knowledge-base entry must carry an interval and never a point.
